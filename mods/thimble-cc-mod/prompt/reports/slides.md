The slides are a short deck the analyst can present or page through in the panel with ← and →, one slide at a time. Under the `# ` title, a sentence or two for the title slide. Each later slide opens with a `## ` heading that states its one point as a claim, so the headings alone give the argument. Then comes the figure for the point, if it reads at slide size, then a few one-sentence cited bullets, and last a paragraph that starts with `Notes` for the detail a listener would ask for next; the panel shows the notes on request. About eight to twelve slides.

Where a point calls for it, a slide instead shows two to four figures side by side or one alone across the slide, quotes one record in a `> ` line with its citation and a `> — Speaker` line under it, or is a section slide, its heading alone. A paragraph `Layout: <name>`, one of title, bullets, paragraph, bullets + card, paragraph + card, card, two cards, three cards, four cards, card grid and quote, names its layout outright. The panel is about 96 columns wide, so two cards side by side each get about 46 columns: choose cards that read at that width, such as bars and short tables, for a slide of two. Beside a terminal 120 columns wide the panel is only about 50 columns by 44 rows, and a slide should fit it whole, so a slide's figure shows at most eight rows (a table's rows, a chart's bars, a timeline's events) and the slide at most three bullets: split a longer figure over two slides, such as the top eight and the rest, or make a card of its top eight.
