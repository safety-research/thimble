## A good view

The analyst uses the view to understand records, often thousands of them, without reading every file. Build it to "overview first, zoom and filter, details on demand". The view opens on the whole of what it covers at a glance, with records in view, so the analyst sees the shape of the data before any detail. They narrow it to what they care about, and any one record opens in full under the overview, so they keep their place.

The rest is your judgment. Aim for the view a demanding designer would ship, one that reads at once without instructions:

- Each tab has the proposal's four parts, which the spec requires. `overview`: a compact visual of every row of the tab, drawn above its records in at most a third of the panel: a strip of counts over time (`histogram`), lanes on a time axis, bars by a category, or a small graph. `zoom`: one short phrase saying what a click on that overview narrows the rows to ("a click on a day narrows to its events"), which the reviewer reads and the panel does not print. `filter`: the fields the analyst narrows by most, a few values each, in one row (the labels join it by themselves), and the text search reads. Details: the collection's `detail`, what one selected row shows under the records, with the rows of other collections that name it.
- The value of a view is the links between files: a message beside the message it answers, a page beside the labels that saved it, a session under the one that spawned it. Join the files into rows that carry those links (`link` fields) and show them in the details (`related`), so one click follows what no single file shows.
- The overview fits the panel, about 96 columns wide and 48 rows high, and 66 columns in a narrower window. When there are more runs, lanes or rows than fit, group or aggregate them (a table grouped by run, a lane per session, bars by kind) rather than leave most of them below the fold.
- The panel colours one field per tab, the overview's colour field (a strip's or lanes' `color`, a bar's `field`), the same way in the overview, its legend, the ● before each of its values in the filter row, and the records and the details, where it is a glyph at each row's left edge; its words stay in the text colour. Pick the category the analyst tells rows apart by, with at most seven values, such as a state or a source.
- Every screen should be skimmable: the analyst sees at a glance what the groups are, which items matter and what state each is in. The panel draws three levels (a group's heading, an item, its secondary text) by position on its grid, dim text and blank lines, colour only on the colour field's glyphs and on flagged values, and no bold, as {{spec_md}} says under "The visual system" and "Visual hierarchy"; your part is what goes into each level. Give a list's `meta` the two or three fields an analyst tells items apart by, and its `text` a short line your reader makes with the facts that matter, such as `#15 by elm · open for 1h 53m · closes #8`, leaving the rest to the details. Order a table's columns by what the analyst needs most, the row's name first: where the panel is too narrow for all of them it leaves the last ones to the details, so a name stays readable. Keep tags (flags, areas, kinds) as category or list fields, in `tags` or `meta`, where the panel draws them as tags, rather than words inside a line of text; keep quantities as number or duration fields, which it right-aligns; declare `flag` on the values that are problems, which it marks in red (× for the first value, ! for the others), and on nothing else.
- Build the view at the proposal's size: the records its unit names, and the links its details ask for. The panel loads every row of every collection at once, so keep each row short. A text field needs no more than the 12 lines the details draw, about 1,000 characters, and a line or two in a collection of thousands of rows, so cut a long one, such as a page's body, a message or a file's contents, to its start, or leave it out: the row's `ref` opens the whole record. The checks fail rows that take more than 10 MB in all, and note rows past 3.5 MB, which are written in parts: aim for one file.
- Scales and sizes come from the data. First read the claimed files, a jsonl file a few lines at a time since a line can be long, and count what the view must fit: the units, the time span, the longest names, how many values each category has.
- A row's title is what a person calls it: never a hex id, a uuid or a hash. Name an agent's session by the agent and its run, a commit by its message. Give names whole: the panel shortens a name that does not fit, keeping its start and its end, and the checks fail names the reader cut.
- Label each field by exactly what its value is, since the labels are all the view says about its numbers: `named labels` for a count that leaves out the unnamed ones, `week first stored` for the week a page was first saved, `body length` for a length in characters. The title row above a bars or strip overview names what it counts from its field's label ("pages by week first stored"), and the selected row's meta values show after their labels. A number that names rather than counts (a line, a sequence number, a year) reads without thousands separators when its name or label says so (`seq`, `line`, `year`, `id`, `number`).
- Name each tab by what its rows are, in words the analyst understands without the other tabs, such as `Revisions` or `Partly coordinated pages`.
- The header's numbers and the filter row agree: the filter row counts a tab's rows in the words of the header's `count` stats of its collection. Keep one row per thing the header counts, such as a mark drawn on several lanes as one row listing them, and count each kind of row a tab draws.
- The view explains nothing in words. The analyst learns it by using it and by the names on its controls, so its `description` is one sentence for the list of views and nothing on the view is a hint or a caption.

## What thimble holds every view to

Code checks these in every view, and the analyst sees the first three above it, so they can always tell what the view covers and what it made of the files.

- **Every claimed file is read or listed.** The checks count the bytes build_index reads of each claimed file through Python's `open()`. Read each claimed file whole, and return a file you leave out on purpose from `hidden(index)` as `[{"path": ..., "why": ...}]`. A file read other than through `open()`, as sqlite3 or mmap read one, is not counted, so open it with `open()` and hand the library its bytes. Folders beside a claimed one that hold the same files, such as other runs, are noted: claim every run and let the analyst choose with a facet.
- **What the reader made is listed.** A field the reader makes rather than reads as the file holds it, such as a time parsed to UTC, a value parsed from text, fields merged or a name looked up, is declared in its collection's `fields` with `"derived": "cleaned"`, or `"computed"` for a value the files do not state, such as a join, an estimate or a classification, and `from` and `how`, short plain phrases without semicolons. The header counts them and lists them on a click. The checks compare sampled rows with the lines they cite and fail on every field whose values those lines do not hold and that is not declared derived. A renamed field, a default for a missing value, a count and the view's own keys need no entry.
- **Lines it cannot parse are reported.** `problems(index)` returns `[{"ref": "<path>#L<n>", "why": ...}]` for each line the reader could not parse; the header shows "N unreadable lines". The checks run the reader on a copy of the files with one file missing and a line cut short: it must keep working there and report the torn line.
- **Labels show on its rows.** A label marks records, such as the messages that ask for help, and the analyst turns labels on and filters by them in the view. The mod draws each label by the rows' refs, so give every row a `ref` in citation form for the record it shows, and leave the spec's label controls on. The checks turn on a test label that marks about one row in seven and fail when a tab draws no mark.
- **The spec draws within the panel.** The checks validate the spec and the rows against it, then draw every tab as it opens and with its first row selected, 96 and 66 columns wide, and fail on a line wider than the panel, more lines than its rows, or a drawing that fails.

## The files

- `reader.py` runs with the folder as its working directory and defines these top-level functions, beside `hidden(index)` and `problems(index)` above. `build_index(paths)` gets the claimed files' paths and returns the index, which the mod caches; it keeps only what finding a record needs when the files are large. `records(index, {"op": "rows"})` returns every row the view draws, as `{"collections": {<name>: [row, ...]}}`, each row a flat JSON object (strings, numbers, booleans, null, or a list of those) with a key unique in its collection and a `ref` (`<path>#L<n>`, `<path>#L<a>-L<b>`, `<csv>#row=<n>`, `<json>#/<pointer>`, `<db>#<table>/<key>`). Past about 50,000 rows, aggregate in the reader, such as a session's calls counted rather than listed. `resolve(index, locator)` gets `{"path", "fragment"}` or `{"key"}` and returns None when the view does not know the place, else `excerpt` (the record's text, word for word), `label` (a few words), `refs` (the `<path>#L<n>` it stands for) and `key` (the unit's key, if any). `unplaced(index)` returns `[{"ref", "why"}]` for records read but not drawn, such as a reference to a row that is not there.
- `view.json` is the spec: its name, slug, description, `scope` (globs relative to the folder: the files the view claims), collections with their fields and details, stats, tabs with their overview, zoom, filter and records, the citation forms `accepts` and `units`. {{spec_md}} has every field and component, what each draws, and the limits; read it before you write the spec.
- Do not write `rows.json`: the check writes it from your reader.

## Checking

Check the view with `{{check}}`, adding as arguments the refs sampling may miss, such as a key of each unit the view gives (`view:{{slug}}/<key>`). It runs every check above in code, writes rows.json, and prints what failed and what it noted. See the view as the analyst will with `{{render}}`, which prints every tab as it opens and with its first row selected; `--tab N`, `--select <collection>/<key>` and `--width 66` draw one state.

When your turn ends, the mod runs the same checks. The view opens in the panel when they pass, and a reviewer then reads the drawn view and may send you problems to fix. Reviewing the view is that reviewer's job, so check it with the command above and start no review or subagent of your own. End your turn with one sentence that says what the view shows, written as your final message rather than handed back through a tool.
