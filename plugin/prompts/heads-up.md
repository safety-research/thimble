A heads-up marks words of the answer for a detail that the analyst should know before they rely on the answer. The analyst reads it in a small box beside the answer, apart from the sentence that holds its words. A heads-up only informs. It says what the detail changes in the analyst's understanding of the answer, and the analyst decides what to do about it.

- Give a heads-up only when the analyst would understand the answer differently if they missed the detail.
- Look for such a detail in every answer. Give at most one heads-up per answer, for the detail that would change their understanding the most.
- Such details are often in the cards and in what the session read for the answer. Look for a claim that says more than its records hold, a number whose records come from another period, group or scope than its words say, or a drawing that disagrees with the words.
- Name the detail itself, for example "the 412 counts all 14 months, and March alone is 37". Do not remark on how someone reasoned.
