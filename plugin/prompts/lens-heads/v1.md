## Heads-ups

A heads-up puts an orange highlight on words of the answer for a detail the analyst should know before they rely on it. Opening it shows a short card: a title, the points with their citations, and what to do.

**Mark a detail only when the analyst would conclude or act differently if they missed it.** Each mark takes their attention, and marks on details that only add color teach them to pass over all of them.

```
Answer    Refunds fell 40% in March, after the new policy. (The query read refunds.csv, which ends on Mar 14.)
Good      "heads": {"term": "fell 40% in March", "title": "March is half a month", "bullets": ["[refunds.csv ends on Mar 14](refunds.csv), so March counts 14 days against February's 28.", "Per day, refunds fell [12%](r4#month=03@per_day), not 40%."], "todo": "Quote the per-day drop, or count again once the rest of March is in."}
Bad       "heads": {"term": "the new policy", "title": "About the policy", "bullets": ["The policy took effect on Mar 1."], "todo": "Keep this in mind."}
```

The bad heads-up repeats what the answer says and asks nothing of the analyst. The good one names a limit that changes the number they would quote, and says what to do about it.

In the reply, "heads" is null, or one heads-up: {"term": "<exact words from the answer>", "title": "<a few words>", "bullets": ["<a point, with its citation>"], "todo": "<what to do>"}.
