## The claims of this turn

The analyst keeps the work's claims in a pane beside the chat: each claim in a cluster, with what it means, the rows it cites, and the claims it bears on. You give this turn's claims. Read the analyst's message and everything Claude wrote in the turn, including its words between tool calls.

**A claim is one statement the analyst could check or act on.** A count, a share, a cause, a comparison, an "all" or a "most", and a reading of the data ("the edits look biased") are claims, also when the analyst states them. A plan or a step of method ("I will load the file") is not, because nothing in it can be wrong. Most turns have two to six claims, and a turn that only plans or asks has none.

**The quote places the claim.** Give 4 to 15 words copied exactly from the message that said it, without the citation's link: for "Alice made [41%](r4#user=Alice@share) of the edits", quote "Alice made 41% of the edits". thimble finds the sentence by the quote and takes its citations as the claim's evidence, so a quote that is not word for word loses the claim. The quote is also the claim's name, so give "claim" only when the quote leans on "it" or "this" and does not say the claim alone.

**Give a part only when the claim's words leave it open.** The analyst reads one claim at a time, and a part that only says the quote again costs them reading and costs this reply. Give "precisely" when the words leave out the quantity, what it is counted out of, the window or how it was measured, and the session shows them. Give "unstated" when the words leave out what it is counted out of or its window and nothing in the session shows it: ask it as a short question, since a guess would mislead. Give "judgment" when a word of the claim is Claude's reading and not a measurement. Give "based_on" only when the sentence cites nothing.

**A claim said again is the same claim, and a correction keeps the old one.** The analyst sees how often a claim was said, and which claim replaced which. When this turn says a claim from "Claims so far" again, in any words, give only "same" with its id and the quote. When it corrects, narrows or replaces one, give the new claim with "revises" and the old id. A claim of this same turn has no id yet: revise it as "#N", its place in your list.

**Clusters name what the work is doing.** The analyst finds a claim by its cluster, so a cluster holds the claims that answer one question the work looks into. Give "cluster": the id of a cluster so far when the claim answers its question, else a new name of two to five plain words, such as "finding who edited" or "ruling out bots". A question about a new subject, such as another person, file or cause, is a new line of work and gets a new cluster. When a cluster is listed as grown, split off the claims that answer another question, or rename it when its name no longer says what it holds. Give "kind": fact (true of the setup), finding (the data shows it), judgment (a reading or a choice where another was possible), event (it happened at a time) or claim (said, and not shown).
