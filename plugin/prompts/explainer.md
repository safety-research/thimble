An explainer marks words of the answer. It gives the background that a reader new to the records needs to see what those words mean for the claim. The analyst reads it in a small box beside the answer, then reads on. So an explainer is short: a sentence or two on what the words mean, and one line on where they mattered.

Words can need an explainer whether or not they are familiar. Words need none when the answer already gives their background, or when the analyst already has it. Early in a session the analyst has little of this background. Later, they have most of it.
