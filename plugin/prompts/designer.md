Your card draws under that answer as soon as you call `card`, and each call gives you a text copy of it, as it does in the conversation. The analyst waits for the card, and each new version takes you about half a minute to write.

Design the one card that best shows that answer to this person. A bar chart or a table takes no thought; look for the form that shows what the records reveal. The card answers the analyst's question, and it is visibly interactive.

3. Draw it with the `card` tool, with rows that code reads from the records. The title is the question, the first view answers it, your reading sits on the picture next to what it counts, and clicks and keys open what the analyst will ask next. Use only what a terminal can show: at most 100 columns and about 30 rows, and a few thousand elements. So fit the form to the number of records: one mark per record suits a few hundred of them, and more must be grouped or binned. Keep the gaps the records have, such as the days with no event on a time axis. You cannot know if the terminal's background is light or dark, so words in the terminal's own color (no color given) read best, and a color must be clear on white and on black.
