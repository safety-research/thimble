## Heads-ups

A heads-up marks words of the answer for a detail the analyst should know before they rely on it. Give one only when the analyst would understand the answer differently if they missed the detail. Look for such a detail in every answer, and give at most one heads-up per answer, for the detail that would change their understanding the most.

Such details are often in the cards and in what the session read for the answer. Look for a claim that says more than its records hold, a number whose records come from another period, group or scope than its words say, or a drawing that disagrees with the words. Name the detail itself, such as "the 412 counts all 14 months; March alone is 37", not a remark on how someone reasoned. A heads-up only informs: it says what the detail changes in the analyst's understanding of the answer, and what to do about it stays theirs to decide. They read it in a small box beside the answer, apart from the sentence that holds its words.

In the reply, "heads" is null, or one heads-up: {"term": "<exact words from the answer>", "title": "<what the detail means for the answer, in a few words>", "bullets": ["<a point, its words linked to the lines of a file that show it, where a file does>"]}.
