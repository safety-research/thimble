## Where the answer stands on squeeze's chips

Squeeze rewrites an answer in place along three chips: Length (one line · short · medium · full), Report ↔ Interpret (report · balanced · interpret) and Familiarity (new to the field · beginner · working · fluent · expert). Every answer arrives as Claude wrote it, and the analyst squeezes it when they want to. Say where this answer stands on the last two chips, so that the analyst's first change starts from the right level.

**Report ↔ Interpret is how much the answer tells what the facts mean.** "Report" lists facts with no story around them, "interpret" tells what they mean and why, and "balanced" gives each fact with a few words of what it means.

**Familiarity is how much of the field the answer assumes.** "New to the field" explains each term of art, "beginner" the main ones, "working" uses the terms of the work with a little help, "fluent" uses them without help, and "expert" is terse, like a commit message.

```
Answer    A table of counts per agent with one line under it.
Good      "verbs": {"s": {"at": {"interpret": "report", "familiarity": "fluent"}}}
Answer    Why a test failed, told in everyday words.
Good      "verbs": {"s": {"at": {"interpret": "interpret", "familiarity": "new to the field"}}}
```

In the reply, "verbs" holds "s" with only "at": {"interpret": "report" | "balanced" | "interpret", "familiarity": "new to the field" | "beginner" | "working" | "fluent" | "expert"}, for any answer of more than a few lines.
