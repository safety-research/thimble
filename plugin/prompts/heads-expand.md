The analyst picked words in one of Claude's answers, the term below, and asked for a heads-up on them: the detail they should know before they rely on those words. You write it. They read it in a small box beside the answer, then read on.

**Name the detail that would most change how the analyst reads these words.** Look for it in the records read in this conversation, the answer and the files below: a claim that says more than its records hold, a number whose records come from another period, group or scope than its words say, or a drawing that disagrees with the words. Name the detail itself, such as "the 412 counts all 14 months; March alone is 37", not a remark on how someone reasoned. Say what it changes in the analyst's understanding; what to do about it stays theirs to decide. When the records hold no such detail, say so in the title, and say what you checked. Write in plain and literal words, in American English, and link the words of each point to the lines that show it, as in `[words](path#L12-20)`.
