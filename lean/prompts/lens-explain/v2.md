## Explain marks

An explain mark puts a teal fill on words in the answer. When the person opens it, they read your explanation of those words in place, without asking Claude and without a longer answer.

**A reader stops at a word they cannot place.** The person knows their question, but often not the field of the data, nor the words the work made up on the way. Two kinds of words stop them:
- a term of art of the field, such as "/16 network", "fixture scope", "p95" or "rebase";
- a name the work coined for a set, a rule or a pattern, such as "overwrite", "the wiper accounts" or "SEC flood", when the answer uses it as if the person knew its rule.

**Mark the words a claim depends on.** When the person reads a term wrong, they read the claim wrong: a reader who takes "overwrite" to mean "delete" misreads every count of overwrites. So mark a term when a claim of the answer turns on it and the answer does not say what it is in the same sentence. Do not mark plain words, numbers, names of files or people, a term the person used themselves, or a term the answer already explains.

**Most answers about unfamiliar data have one or two such words; some have none.** Mark the one to three the person is most likely to stop at, and none when there are none.

- The **term** is the exact words as they stand in the answer, so thimble can find them.
- The **body** says what the term is, in one or two plain sentences. For a name the work coined, give its rule as the work applied it.
- **"here"** says where the term mattered in this work, in one sentence, as a fact the person can check, with citations from the turn written as `[words](ref)`.
- A **program** is optional. Give one only when a tiny drawing says it better than words, such as a contrast of two cases side by side. It is the body of a function `(k, data) => node` in thimble's drawing toolkit, at most six lines, such as `return k.beside(k.text('**scope="module"** {dim:one cart per file}'), k.text('**default** {dim:a fresh cart per test}'), { gap: 4 })`.
A term the person already saw explained tells them nothing. Do not repeat a term listed under "Terms the person already saw explained".

These examples come from other data than this session's. They show the reasoning, not words to copy.

**A term of art and a coined name.** The answer says: "From June 3 the canary ran at p95 above 800 ms on 6 of 9 hosts, so the rollout paused. I call a host hot when its p95 stayed over 800 ms for an hour; 4 hosts were hot." "p95" is a term of art the claim turns on. "canary" is one too. "hot" is coined, but the answer gives its rule in the same breath, so it needs no mark:
`"glosses": [{"term": "p95", "body": "The time under which 95 of every 100 requests finish: the slowest 5 in 100 take longer.", "here": "It stayed above 800 ms on [6 of 9 hosts](q2#over=800ms@hosts) from June 3."}, {"term": "canary", "body": "A new version sent to a few hosts first, so a problem shows before every host has it."}]`

**Nothing to mark.** An answer in plain words that counts rows of a file the person named, with each term explained where it stands: `"glosses": []`.

In the reply, "glosses" holds the explain marks, zero to three: [{"term": "<exact words from the answer>", "body": "<what it is, one or two sentences>", "here": "<where it mattered in this work, one sentence with citations>", "program": "<optional, at most six lines>"}].
