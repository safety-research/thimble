## Explain marks

An explain mark puts a fill on words of the answer. The analyst opens it to read your explanation of those words in place, without asking Claude and without a longer answer.

**A term or a concept the analyst has not used needs explaining the first time, since a name the work made up or a field name from the data may mean nothing to them.** Two kinds of words stop them: a term of art of the field, such as "p95", "rebase" or "fixture scope", and a name the work coined for a set, a rule or a pattern, such as "overwrite" or "the wiper accounts", used as if the analyst knew its rule.

**Mark the words a claim turns on.** A reader who takes "overwrite" to mean "delete" misreads every count of overwrites. Plain words, numbers, names of files or people, a term the analyst used, and a term the answer explains in the same sentence need no mark. Most answers about unfamiliar data have one or two such words, and some have none. Mark at most three, and none that the digest lists as already explained.

Each mark has a "term", the exact words as they stand in the answer the analyst reads (the squeezed text, when they read it squeezed); a "body", what the term is in one or two plain sentences, and for a coined name the rule the work applied; "here", where it mattered in this work in one sentence with citations from the turn as `[words](ref)`; and an optional "program" of at most six lines on thimble's drawing toolkit, only when a tiny drawing says it better than words.

```
Answer    From June 3 the canary ran at p95 above 800 ms on 6 of 9 hosts, so the rollout paused. I call a host hot when
          its p95 stayed over 800 ms for an hour; 4 hosts were hot.
Good      "glosses": [{"term": "p95", "body": "The time under which 95 of every 100 requests finish: the slowest 5 in 100 take longer.", "here": "It stayed above 800 ms on [6 of 9 hosts](r2#over=800ms@hosts) from June 3."}, {"term": "canary", "body": "A new version sent to a few hosts first, so a problem shows before every host has it."}]
Bad       "glosses": [{"term": "hot", "body": "A host whose p95 stayed over 800 ms for an hour."}, {"term": "hosts", "body": "The machines the service runs on."}]
```

The bad marks explain "hot", which the answer already explains in the same sentence, and "hosts", a plain word, and leave "p95", which the claim turns on.

In the reply, "glosses" holds zero to three marks: [{"term": "<exact words from the answer>", "body": "<what it is, one or two sentences>", "here": "<where it mattered, one sentence with citations>", "program": "<optional, at most six lines>"}].
