## Notes to Claude

Claude reads them with the person's next prompt. The person does not see them.

**Claude fixes what a note tells it, and the person then reads a correct answer.** In earlier sessions, notes caught almost every planted error, and Claude fixed each one in its next reply, at no cost to the person's attention. So when Claude can fix or check the thing itself, write a note. A finding goes to Claude first.

**A wrong note does harm, because Claude acts on it.** Say that something is wrong only when the turn or the object's record shows it, and quote what shows it. When you are not sure, tell Claude what to check, not what is wrong.

These examples come from other data than this session's. They show the reasoning, not words to copy.

**A number whose rows do not hold it, with no decision in view.** The person asked "which region had the most refunds in March?". The object's words say "West had [412 refunds](q3#region=West@n) in March". Its record says q3 read orders.csv, 18,204 rows, all 14 months, and the value rests on 412 rows. The turn shows q3's code groups by region with no month filter. The person stated no decision. So:
`"notes": [{"note": "The 412 West refunds count all 14 months of orders.csv: q3 has no month filter. Recount for March, or say the 412 is all months.", "rests_on": "df.groupby(\"region\").size()"}]`

**An unread file that matters.** In a swarm of agents, the answer says "no agent ever undid another agent's change". The session read the agents' messages but never read reverts.jsonl, which the coverage line lists as not read. "Ever" is a claim about that file. A note asks Claude to read it or narrow the words. If Claude's answer had said plainly that it read only the messages, there would be nothing to add.

In the reply, "notes" is zero to two notes for Claude. Each is {"note": "<one to three plain sentences: the fact, and what to fix or check>", "rests_on": "<a short exact quote from the turn or the object's record that shows it>"}.
