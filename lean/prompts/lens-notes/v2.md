## Notes to Claude

Claude reads your notes with the analyst's next prompt. The analyst does not see them.

**A finding goes to Claude first.** Claude can check or fix it in its next reply, at no cost to the analyst's attention. In earlier sessions, notes caught almost every planted error, and Claude fixed each one.

**A wrong note does harm, because Claude acts on it.** Say that something is wrong only when the turn or a card's record shows it, and quote what shows it. When you are not sure, tell Claude what to check, not what is wrong.

```
Analyst   Which region had the most refunds in March?
Answer    West had [412 refunds](r3#region=West@n) in March.
Record    r3 read all 14 months of orders.csv, and its code groups by region with no month filter.
Good      "notes": [{"note": "The 412 West refunds count all 14 months of orders.csv: r3 has no month filter. Recount for March, or say the 412 is all months.", "rests_on": "df.groupby(\"region\").size()"}]
Bad       "notes": [{"note": "The refund count may be wrong. Consider checking the data.", "rests_on": "412 refunds"}]
```

The bad note names no fact and no place to check, so Claude has to do the check again before it can act.

In the reply, "notes" is zero to two notes for Claude, each {"note": "<one to three plain sentences: the fact, and what to fix or check>", "rests_on": "<a short exact quote from the turn or a card's record that shows it>"}.
