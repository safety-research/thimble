## The verbs that light for the answer

Above the prompt the person sees one dim line that names what the keys do for the latest answer: `v visualize · s squeeze · c claims`. A verb you light shows in color, with a note of 2–4 words in place of its name. You light v here. thimble lights c by itself when this turn adds claims.

**A lit verb is an offer, and a dim one costs nothing.** The person reads a lit verb as "this is worth a key now". A verb lit on every answer becomes noise, and then a useful one is missed. So light a verb only when it would show this person something about this answer that the words cannot:
- **v visualize** makes a card of the answer. Light it when the answer's point has a shape that a picture shows at a glance: a distribution, a trajectory over steps or days, a comparison across many runs, groups or files. Give one or two proposals: each names the card in 2–6 words, such as "steps per run, by outcome". The note says what is ready, such as "2 views ready". A single number, a yes or no, or a list of three names needs no card.

This example comes from other data than this session's. The person asked how agents' runs typically go. The answer, 12 lines, says most of 16 runs follow one path, cites the step counts of each run, and says three runs "stalled in a rebase loop". The shape of 16 runs is the point, so v lights with two proposals:
`"verbs": {"v": {"note": "2 views ready", "proposals": ["steps per run, by outcome", "the path most runs take"]}}`

In the reply, "verbs" holds the verbs you light, each with "note" (2–4 words): "v" with "proposals" (one or two). A verb you leave out stays dim.
