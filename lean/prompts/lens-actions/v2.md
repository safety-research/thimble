## The actions that light for the answer

Above the prompt the person sees one dim line that names what the keys do for the latest answer: `v visualize · s squeeze · c claims`. An action you light shows in color, with a note of 2–4 words in place of its name. You light v here. thimble lights c by itself when this turn adds claims.

**A lit action is an offer, and a dim one costs nothing.** The person reads a lit action as "this is worth a key now". An action lit on every answer becomes noise, and then a useful one is missed. So light one only when it would show this person something about this answer that the words cannot.

**v is worth lighting when a claim in the answer would be clearer seen than read, and no card under the answer shows it yet.** Answers often state a shape in words: a burst on one day, a split between groups, a run that loops, a share that rests on a threshold, many runs that end differently. Read the answer's claims one at a time and ask which one the person would grasp faster as a picture. The objects list the cards the answer already has: a view must show something they do not, such as the same claim from another side, or one part of it up close. A single number, a yes or no, or a list of three names needs no card.

**A proposal is the card, named.** It says what the card shows and how, in 3–8 words, in the answer's own names: "overwrites per day, June 18 marked", "AgentRelent's edits by network". The person decides from the name alone whether to press v, and Claude draws from it. A name that fits any answer ("a chart of the data", "summary view") tells them nothing. Give one or two proposals, the clearest first.

These examples come from other data than this session's. They show the reasoning, not words to copy.

**A claim with a shape the answer only tells.** The answer says: "Failures cluster late: 41 of 52 came after minute 300, and r9 failed 12 times in a row while its disk was full." Its card is a table of failures per run, which does not show when they came. So:
`"verbs": {"v": {"note": "2 views ready", "proposals": ["failures by minute, runs stacked", "r9's 12 failures against disk use"]}}`

**A claim the card already shows.** The answer says June 18 had the most saves, and its card is saves per day with June 18 marked. A second view of the same days adds nothing, so v stays dim, unless the answer also makes a claim the card does not show, such as who made the saves.

In the reply, "verbs" holds the actions you light, each with "note" (2–4 words): "v" with "proposals" (one or two names of cards). An action you leave out stays dim.
