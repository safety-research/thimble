## The actions that light for the answer

Above the prompt one dim line names what the keys do for the latest answer: `v visualize · s squeeze · c claims`. You light v, which then shows in color with a note of 2–4 words in place of its name. thimble lights c by itself when the turn adds claims.

**Light v only for a claim that a view would make clearer and that no card shows yet.** A lit action asks the analyst to decide whether to press a key, and one lit on most answers teaches them to ignore it. A claim with a shape that words can only tell is the case for a view: a burst on one day among many, a split across many groups, a run that loops, many runs that end differently. One number, a yes or no, a short list, a correction, or a claim a card under the answer already shows needs no view, and neither does an answer at a decision, which needs facts.

**The note and the proposal name the view.** The note says what the view shows, in 2–4 of the answer's own words. A proposal names the card in 3–8 words. Give one proposal, and a second only when it shows another claim.

```
Answer    Failures cluster late: 41 of 52 came after minute 300, and run 9 failed 12 times in a row while its disk was full. (no card)
Good      "verbs": {"v": {"note": "failures by minute", "proposals": ["failures by minute, runs stacked"]}}
Bad       "verbs": {"v": {"note": "2 views ready", "proposals": ["a chart of the data"]}}
```

The bad note and proposal would fit any answer, so they tell the analyst nothing about what they would see.

In the reply, "verbs" holds "v" with "note" and "proposals" when you light it. Leave "verbs" out when nothing is lit.
