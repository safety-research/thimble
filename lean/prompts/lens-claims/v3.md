## The claims of this turn

The analyst follows the work through its claims: a graph groups them by what the work is doing, and when the analyst points at a claim, it shows what exactly the claim means. Give this turn's claims, from the analyst's message and everything Claude wrote after it, including the short texts between its tool calls.

**A claim is one statement that can be true or false.** "June 18 had 6,543 saves" is a claim; "I will load the file now" is not. A count, a share, a cause, a comparison, an "all" or a "most", a reading of the data, and a statement the analyst makes about the work are claims. A step of method with no result is not.

**Each claim says where it was said, in its own words.** "quote" is 4 to 15 words copied exactly from the message, without the citation's markdown, so thimble can find the sentence and its citations. The quote is also the claim's name, so give "claim" only when the quote does not say the claim by itself, such as when it leans on "it" or "this". Give "by": "person" for a claim in the analyst's message (the value is a code word).

**The analyst must be able to tell what exactly a claim means.** "480 reruns were extra" does not say extra compared with what, out of how many, or over which steps. So give each new claim "precisely": the claim restated so that it can be checked, in at most 20 words, with its quantity, what it is counted out of, its window, and how it was measured. A set the work named needs its rule in a few words. When the sentence cites nothing, give "based_on": the file, the records picked and how many, in at most 12 words, or "the analyst's words; nothing cited". When a word is Claude's reading and not a measurement, give "judgment": the word, and why it is a reading. When the words leave out the denominator or the window, give "unstated": the gap as a fact when the work shows it, else a short question.

**A claim said again is the same claim, and a correction revises it.** When this turn says a claim so far again, give only {"same": "<its id>", "quote": "..."}. When it corrects, narrows or replaces one (a new count, a wrong cause), give the new claim with "revises": "<the old id>", or "#N" for the Nth claim of this same turn. A claim that adds detail is a new claim.

**Clusters show what the work is doing.** A cluster holds the claims about one thing the work looks into, named in two to five plain words, such as "the June 18 burst". Give "cluster": the id of a cluster so far when one fits, else a new name. A cluster listed as grown can get a better name, or a split when it holds two lines of work.
