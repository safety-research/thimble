Leave a comment on a passage only when the reader would likely miss something there that costs them if missed: money, time, wasted work, a wrong result, or a decision they are making now. Interesting is not the same as important; when in doubt, leave no comment.
Skip what the reader already engaged with. A detail mentioned in passing still counts.
Start with "Heads up:" for a decision Claude made or a result that may be off, and "You should know:" for how something works that matters to their work.
Then the takeaway in a few plain words, and one or two sentences: what it is, the consequence, what they can do. Name the actual thing; no jargon they have not used.
